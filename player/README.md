# Neo Geo Media Centre: player source

You do not need this folder to use the Foundry: the finished player is already built into it. This is the source, for anyone who wants to change the player itself.

Files here replace the ones of the same name in a copy of ngdevkit's `15-sound-adpcmb` example.

## Build it

Install ngdevkit (see its own instructions), then:

    cd ngdevkit-examples
    cp -r 15-sound-adpcmb ngmc-player
    rm -rf ngmc-player/build

Copy this folder's contents (keep `assets` and `tools`) over `ngmc-player`, replacing main.c, user_commands.s and the Makefile. Then:

    cd ngmc-player
    make
    make gngeo

The emulator should show the brass "NEO GEO MEDIA CENTRE" screen with "NO MUSIC ON THIS CARTRIDGE". That is correct: the music is added by the Foundry.

To use your own build in the Foundry, run `make foundry-input` and swap the result in with `tools/embed-player.py`.

## What is in the box

- `main.c`: 68k player (list, play, stop, repeat, prev/next, progress bar) with some life on
  the screen: turning cogs, two chain belts that slide in opposite directions and back again,
  a white sweep along the progress bar every few seconds, a glint across the title now and
  then, and long track names that scroll in the list
- `assets/cogs.fix`: ready-made extra fix-layer tiles (chain belts and cogs), added to the S ROM by
  the Makefile. `tools/gen_tiles.py` makes them again if you want to redraw them.
- `tools/screen-mockup.png`: a mock-up of the screen, drawn from the real tile data
- `user_commands.s`: Z80 sound commands, generated by `tools/gen_user_commands.py`
  (command 4 stops, command 5+n plays track n, up to 64 tracks)
- `Makefile`: the example's own, with the ROM name and title changed

Keep `MAX_TRACKS` the same (64) in `main.c`, in the generator and in the Foundry.

## Themes and screensavers (v4)

main.c holds a 220-byte theme table (marker `NGMC-THEM-01`) that the Foundry patches into the P ROM
when it builds a cartridge, exactly like the track table. It holds the 11 palettes, backdrop colour,
the screen title (20 characters), animation tempo (cog and belt speed, idle and playing), the
screensaver mode and the idle delay. The defaults in main.c are the original brass look, so a
cartridge that was never patched still looks right. No graphics are added, so audio space is unchanged.

Screensavers (all fix-layer and palette tricks, about 1 KB of code between them):
Dim, Partial dim (the now-playing lines stay lit), Bouncing title (when the Foundry has drawn the title's letters into the C ROM it is made of sprites: pixel-smooth glide every frame, rippling letters, skin-coloured colour flow and three fading echoes; otherwise the older tile-by-tile version), Starfield, Warp starfield (hardware-scaled sprite stars flying at you) (palette cycling),
Game of Life, Glyph rain, Light show (glowing orbs on sprites tracing a big Lissajous figure, four turning rosettes and ripples, colours taken from the skin), Gold lettering (the word NEOGEO in big polished gold letters that flip one after another in a wave while a shine sweeps across, with sparkles round the edges), and Surprise me. Any button wakes the screen; that press is not passed on.

Fix tile layout: 0..255 text, 256 and 512 tall title halves, 1280 blank, 1281..1288 belt, 1289..1320 cogs,
1321..1335 solid shades, 1336..1344 and 1345..1353 cobwebs for the top corners. `tools/gen_tiles.py` regenerates the cog and belt tile file.
Previews of the skins and screensavers are in `tools/`.

## Extra sprites (v5)

Hardware sprites (art in the C ROM) and a banner on the fix layer appear now and then while certain tracks play. The art and
the trigger table are pre-built into `assets/x.c1/.c2` (C-ROM tiles, format verified byte for byte against ngdevkit's logo tiles)
and `xdata.h` (tile numbers, palettes, scrambled trigger words); the generator for them is not part of this package. The engine is
`gx_update()` in main.c. To test it, build with a higher `GX_CHANCE` and a shorter `GX_ROLL_FRAMES` added to CFLAGS (or edit the two
defines), then put them back. Theme byte 21 bit 0 switches the extras on or off. ngdevkit's logo tiles stay at tiles 0..255 of the C ROM
(the BIOS splash needs them; the Makefile pads the logo to 256 tiles), so the extra, ambient and light-show orb art starts at tile 256.

## Title sprites (theme version 2)

The sprite tile generator reserves 20 blank C-ROM tiles (`TTL_TILE0` in `xdata.h`); the theme table ends with `title_tile` (offset 220)
holding that number. The Foundry draws each letter of the cartridge title into those tiles on export and sets bit 7 of `saver_mode`;
the player masks it with `SAVER_MODE`. A cartridge that was never through the Foundry keeps the tile-based bounce.

## Gold lettering (pre-rendered)

`gen_spin.py` (numpy, scipy, Pillow; its output is included but the script is not) renders the word in Poppins Bold as inflated gold letters, 64 frames, cut into sprite
tiles: `assets/spin.c1/.c2` (appended after the extra sprite art in the C ROM), `spin_data.h`, and `assets/spin.json` for the Foundry preview.
The generated files are included.

## Title page, start menu and options

After the ngdevkit logo the player shows a glossy title page: the NGMC lettering in gold with a shine that sweeps across it, two turning brass gears, a glowing horizon and twinkling stars. Any button opens the start menu (START NGMC or OPTIONS). The options page sets the screensaver (Off, Dim, Partial dim, Bouncing title, Starfield, Warp starfield, Game of Life, Glyph rain, Light show, Gold lettering, Random) and how long the machine sits idle before it starts (30 seconds, 1, 2, 5 or 10 minutes). The choices start from the cartridge's own look settings and are kept in RAM only, so they reset when the machine is switched off.

Music starts by itself about a second after power-on and carries on behind the title page, the menus and the options. From the player, Start goes back to the menu (the music is not interrupted) and START NGMC returns to the player. B stops the music, and there is no confirm box any more.

The art is made by `tools/gen_title.py` (needs numpy, scipy and Pillow) and lives in the C ROM after the gold lettering (`assets/title.c1`/`.c2`, tile numbers in `title_data.h`). See `title_flow()` in main.c.

## Skins with their own artwork

A skin can also swap the fix-layer tile set (tiles 1280..1353 of the S ROM: belts, cogs, solid blocks, webs).
`tools/gen_tiles.py` writes the default set (`assets/cogs.fix`) and the Halloween set, and puts both in
`assets/fixsets.json` for the Foundry, which patches the chosen set into the cartridge's S ROM at export
(only after checking that the ROM really holds this tile layout there). Theme byte 21 bits 4-7 pick an ambient
effect (0 decide from the screen title, 1 none, 2 and up a specific effect); the Foundry always writes an explicit value.

## Shuffle, name typing, winter skin (v6)
- Button D toggles shuffle ("SHUFFLE" shows by the time). A restarts the shuffle round.
- The track name is typed in letter by letter when a track starts.
- Winter Workshop skin: candy-cane belts, rotating snowflake and bauble artwork.
- The Foundry reads title and artist tags from audio files for track names.

## Changes in player 04
- Warp starfield replaces Plasma (saver id 5): about 140 stars, scaled by the hardware so they grow and brighten as they come nearer. Needs one extra solid tile in the C ROM (made by tools/gen_spin.py).
- Light show is much lighter on the 68000: 16 bit multiplies, no divisions in the loops, only changed sprite values are written, and the rosettes and ripples alternate frames. A held button now wakes any screensaver, so a press cannot be lost if a frame runs long.
- Playlist belts are 38 columns wide (columns 1 to 38), centred and as wide as the text.

## Changes in player 05
- Warp starfield reworked: stars appear at random places and moments, drift out slowly and then rush past as they get nearer. Two extra C ROM tiles, made by tools/gen_spin.py.

## Changes in player 06
- New title page, start menu and options page replace the old brass splash (above). About 4560 more C ROM tiles (assets/title.c1 and title.c2), so the Makefile now appends them to the C ROM files.
- B with nothing playing opens a "RETURN TO MENU?" box. The help line reads "A PLAY  B STOP  C REPEAT  D SHUFFLE".
- The screensaver choice and delay are runtime values (opt_saver, opt_secs in main.c), starting from the theme.
- Player 07: the RETURN TO MENU? box now floats over the playlist instead of taking over the whole screen.
- Player 08 (test): Gilded layout, switched on by theme byte 21 bit 1 (the Foundry's Gilded skin). The title page art sits dimmed behind the player screen: small gold NGMC lettering instead of the title row, turning gears in the top corners, glowing horizon and stars. Extras and ambient effects are off in this layout. Falls back to the old look if that bit is clear.
- Player 09: a new page on the options screen with an ON/OFF switch per extra, once it has appeared. RAM only, so it resets at power-off.
- Player 10: fix for leftover tile data showing under shrunk one-tile sprites (stars on the title page, warp stars, light show orbs). On the hardware a vertically shrunk sprite also draws the tiles below its first one from its tile list, so those entries are now filled with an empty tile (TT_BLANK, made by tools/gen_title.py; see blank_below() in main.c).
- Player 11: fix for grey stripes on real hardware when leaving the start menu for the player (backlog 45, not seen in MAME). The player screen is now built in the dark and fades in, and fades use a lookup table instead of multiplying, so a fade step no longer takes longer than a screen frame. Answering NO in the Return to Menu box is unchanged.
- Player 12: second try at the grey bars during the player's fade-in (backlog 45; player 11 changed them from horizontal to diagonal). The player now stays dark for about half a fade before the colours come up, and each fade step writes its colours in three pieces, one per screen frame, each right after the frame starts, instead of all at once part way down the picture. A test: needs a hardware look.
- Player 13 (test): three new full-screen skin layouts, chosen by theme byte 21 bits 1-3 (1 Gilded, 2 MVS Cabinet, 3 Neon Horizon, 4 Aurora). Each is built from hardware sprites behind the normal player screen, with the art in `assets/skins.c1/c2` (pre-built; only `tools/skin_mvs.py` and its helpers are included) and the layer data in `skins_data.h`.
  - MVS Cabinet: black steel arcade cabinet with a marquee of chasing bulbs in the four button colours (faster while a track plays), a joystick ball that leans with your d-pad and four lettered buttons A to D that light up for play, stop, repeat and shuffle and flash when you press them.
  - Neon Horizon: a setting sun with slits sliding down it, wireframe mountains, a glowing grid floor scrolling towards you, twinkling stars and the odd visitor.
  - Aurora: three swaying curtains of northern lights over a pine forest, twinkling stars.
  Extras and ambient effects are off in these layouts, like Gilded. Screensavers hide the layout and it comes back when the screen wakes. The Foundry (14 or newer) previews all three and falls back to the normal layout if the player ROM is older.
- Player 15 (test): the MVS Cabinet is rebuilt as a photo-style cabinet: orange marquee with an NGMC plate and a dancing equaliser, a black bezel with the screen recess fitted to the playlist, a symmetrical red and white control panel with two sticks and buttons A (red), B (yellow), C (green) and D (blue). Player 1's stick and buttons move on screen when you use them. This layout uses a small hand-drawn font (fix tiles 1354 to 1417) for the now-playing box and help line, and has no belt bars. The occasional extras in the Neon Horizon and Aurora layouts are much rarer. Some trigger words and labels are now stored scrambled in the source, and some art generators are no longer included (their output is).
- Player 16 (test): (1) Playback starts by itself after power-on and carries on through the title page, start menu and options (`play_tick()` runs from the player loop and from every title page frame). Start goes back to the menu without a confirm box; the old RETURN TO MENU? box is gone. Start is read from the BIOS register at 0x10FDAD (P1 and P2 Start): this address has not been checked on real hardware. (2) A SKIN row on the options page switches between the cartridge's own look and up to eight original colour skins at run time. The themes live in `ngmc_alt[8]` and the list in `ngmc_skins` (the Foundry fills both in). The options page now has five rows and its menu plates use sprites 181 and up. (3) The default theme title reads CENTRE.
- Player 17 (test): (1) MVS Cabinet: the playlist and now-playing windows are narrowed to sit inside the cabinet's symmetric panel edge (list rows are 36 columns there instead of 37); the A PLAY / B STOP / C REPEAT / D SHUFFLE strip is spaced evenly; the buttons no longer light up, they only move; the marquee sweep happens about once every ten seconds (separate equaliser layer keeps dancing). (2) The title page, start menu, options and extras pages dim to the Dim level after a minute with no presses. Any press wakes them (and does nothing else); the music keeps playing. This is separate from the player's own screensaver option and does not touch it.
- Player 18 (test): (1) The playlist text of Aurora and Neon Horizon is 36 columns wide, so it sits inside the text panel like the MVS Cabinet's. (2) Neon Horizon's twinkling stars no longer land on the sun. (3) On the title page, start menu and options the twinkling stars are behind the turning cogs (sprite order: backdrop, stars, cogs, lettering; the cogs, lettering and stars moved to sprites 60-112). (4) The title page's second line reads HOME-BREW MUSIC PLAYER so every line is exactly centred (an odd-length line cannot be centred on the 8-pixel text grid).
- Player 19 (test): bit 7 of the theme's belt_idle byte (offset 19) makes the two belts use the Rust palette instead of the Brass one; the Foundry sets it for Winter Workshop so its belts stay red and white while the text is icy blue. The low 7 bits are still the belt speed.
- Player 20 (test): Neon Horizon and Aurora: the playlist header and rows start one row lower (header on row 8, list rows 9-17, so nine tracks show at once instead of ten) and sit inside the text box. Neon Horizon's saucer is redrawn about seven pixels tall and flies at a fixed height in the gap between the title and the top band, so it no longer passes behind the band.
- Player 21 (test): the skin picker carries all twelve skins (the packaged one plus eleven others: `ngmc_alt[11]`, names for 12). Layout skins (Gilded, MVS Cabinet, Neon Horizon, Aurora) can be picked at run time, and each look has its own fix-layer art: the 74-tile sets (standard at tile 1280, Halloween's at 1418, Winter Workshop's at 1492) sit side by side in the S ROM and a look picks its set with bits 6-7 of its cog_play byte (theme version 3; `fxo()`). Cobwebs always come from Halloween's set. Moving between two layouts resets the layout's movers.
- Player 22 (test): the Game of Life screensaver is now an 80 x 56 board of rounded dots (one fix tile holds 2 x 2 cells; tools/gen_tiles.py makes the 625 tile variants at tile 1566, after the Halloween and Winter fix sets), with age colours (newborn white, young amber, old deep orange) and a dim red trail for two generations after a cell dies. It runs at half its old speed (a generation every ten frames, worked out eight board rows per frame). Uses palette 11, which the player screen does not use. assets/cogs.fix is now 29 KB (the zero gap is where the Foundry writes the Halloween and Winter sets).
- Player 23 (test): fix for grey noise on real hardware while the screen fades or dims (backlog 79; never seen in MAME). The palette RAM can only be written safely in the short gap between frames, but the title, menu and options pages rewrote about 900 colours in one frame on every fade step, the menu Dim wrote its colours part way down the picture, the Gilded player screen rewrote 60 palettes in one piece, and waking from a screensaver rewrote everything at once. Now no frame writes more than 8 palettes (128 colours): the title, menu and options pages and the Gilded and layout screens fade a few palettes per frame (fades on those pages move in steps of two eighths and take a little longer), the menu Dim fades down instead of jumping, waking a dimmed screen brings the colours back a piece per frame, and waking from an animated screensaver rebuilds the screen in the dark and fades up. The brightness calculation is also faster (two table lookups per colour instead of three, and none at full brightness). The colours reached are identical to player 22. Needs a hardware look.
